The Problem

Exceptional coffee,
invisible producers.

The DRC's Kivu region sits at the heart of the Great Rift Valley — volcanic soils, consistent rainfall, altitudes stretching to 2 000 metres. The cooperatives here grow Bourbon and Arabica scoring above 85 points. The same benchmark as Ethiopia and Rwanda.

Yet their presence in the global specialty market has been near-invisible. Not because the quality isn't there. Because the infrastructure of visibility wasn't.

Buyers received static PDFs — no search, no filter, no history

No shareable profile for trade forums or buyer meetings

No structured way for a buyer to request a sample from a specific coop

"Specialty coffee: looking beyond quality to markets and structures — the Best of Congo case."
— Busan International Coffee Forum, 2026
The Solution

A digital identity for every cooperative.

A permanent URL and QR code. No login required for buyers. Full profiles in French and English — radar charts, production history, social impact data — accessible on any device in under two seconds.

Shareable Profiles

Every coop gets a permanent URL and print-ready QR code. One link loads the full profile — region, altitude, certifications, sensory chart — on any device, instantly.

Sensory Radar

Aroma, acidity, body, sweetness, aftertaste — rendered as an interactive radar chart from professional cupping data. Buyers see the cup before they taste it.

Member Demographics

Total members, gender split, youth share, household count. The social-impact layer that development finance and impact buyers require before committing.

Best of Congo History

Multi-year competition scores, quantities sold, and buyer names displayed as a year-by-year chart. Not just a score — a verifiable trajectory.

Sample Request Pipeline

A structured form captures buyer details and interest type, routing qualified leads directly into the Congo Agri Platform sourcing pipeline.

Bilingual — FR / EN

Full French and English with automatic browser language detection. Every buyer sees the platform in their language without toggling a switch.

Revenue Model

Four ways the platform makes money.

CongoFarmers monetises at every layer of the specialty coffee supply chain — from cooperative management tools to institutional data licenses.

Cooperative SaaS

Coop Portal Subscriptions

Cooperative managers pay a monthly fee for access to the Coop Portal — profile editing, production data entry, analytics, and QR code generation. Priced for African SMEs.

Monthly recurring · per cooperative
Buyer Intelligence

Premium Roaster Tools

Specialty roasters and importers subscribe to advanced features: real-time harvest alerts, side-by-side comparison engine, cupping score API, and sourcing history. One seat covers an entire buying team.

Annual subscription · per buyer organisation
Sample Commission

Trade Introduction Fee

When a sample request submitted through the platform converts into a confirmed commercial trade, CongoFarmers receives a percentage commission. Tracked end-to-end through the Firestore pipeline.

Success-based · per confirmed transaction
Data Licensing

Institutional Analytics

NGOs, development finance institutions, and government ministries license aggregated origin analytics — production trends, cooperative growth, gender inclusion metrics — to guide programme funding decisions.

Annual SLA · per institution
The Market

The last great undiscovered origin.

Congo is one of the last underdeveloped origin stories in specialty coffee. The quality exists. The cooperatives exist. The competition infrastructure exists. What has been missing is the digital layer that connects all three to the world.

A cooperative selling at commodity price receives $1.50–2.00 per pound. With a verified competition score and a visible profile, that same cooperative can command $4–8 per pound.

The difference is not in the cup. The difference is in the infrastructure of trust.
